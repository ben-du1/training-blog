Why Touching Reciepts is Bad

You paid for your groceries. But at what cost to your endocrine system? WAKE UP. Every single day, millions of unsuspecting citizens walk into grocery stores, purchase their government-approved produce, and willingly accept a small, chemically coated piece of paper from a smiling cashier. They call it a receipt. I call it an unnecessary source of endocrine-disrupting chemical exposure. And unlike what the mainstream establishment might have you believe, there is actual scientific literature behind this one. EXHIBIT A: THE PAPER DOESN'T EVEN USE INK Have you ever wondered how receipts are printed without ink cartridges? Thermal receipt printers use heat to activate chemicals coating the paper. Historically, one of the most common chemicals used for this purpose has been bisphenol A, otherwise known as BPA. BPA is an endocrine disruptor capable of interfering with the body's hormonal signaling, including by mimicking estrogen. That's right. You went into Costco for a rotisserie chicken and walked out with a potential hormone-altering chemical on your fingertips. EXHIBIT B: YOUR SKIN IS NOT A GOVERNMENT-CERTIFIED CHEMICAL BARRIER In 2010, researchers discovered that holding a BPA-containing thermal receipt for just five seconds transferred approximately one microgram of BPA onto dry fingers. With wet or greasy fingers, the amount transferred was approximately ten times higher. FIVE SECONDS. That's less time than it takes the cashier to ask whether you want to sign up for their rewards program. And you thought the rewards program was the suspicious part. EXHIBIT C: THE HAND SANITIZER RABBIT HOLE This is where things get absolutely ridiculous. A 2014 study published in PLOS ONE found that, under specific experimental conditions, handling a receipt for 60 seconds immediately after applying a particular hand sanitizer resulted in 185 times more BPA transferring onto the hand compared with dry hands. ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FIVE TIMES. Researchers also demonstrated that BPA could transfer from contaminated fingers onto French fries, resulting in increased BPA levels in participants' bodies. The French fries were compromised. I REPEAT. THE FRENCH FRIES WERE COMPROMISED. EXHIBIT D: THE BPA-FREE INDUSTRIAL LOOPHOLE "But my receipts are BPA-free!" Congratulations. Allow me to introduce you to bisphenol S, otherwise known as BPS. Manufacturers have frequently substituted BPA with BPS, a structurally similar chemical that also exhibits endocrine-disrupting properties. They changed a letter and expected everyone to go home. BPA-free does not necessarily mean bisphenol-free. Read that again. EXHIBIT E: THE CASHIER IS ON THE FRONT LINES While the average citizen occasionally handles receipts, cashiers may handle hundreds throughout their working day. Repeated occupational exposure is a legitimate scientific concern, and environmental agencies have recommended protective measures for workers who regularly handle thermal paper. Even the Minnesota Pollution Control Agency recommends protective gloves or silicone finger coverings for employees handling certain receipts. THIS IS PUBLICLY AVAILABLE INFORMATION. HOW TO ESCAPE THE RECEIPT-INDUSTRIAL COMPLEX Decline unnecessary paper receipts. Request digital receipts whenever possible. Wash your hands with soap and water before eating. Avoid handling thermal receipts with wet or greasy hands. Stop unnecessarily crumpling receipts or rubbing them between your fingers. Choose bisphenol-free thermal paper when purchasing receipt rolls. Now, scientifically speaking, touching a single receipt does not guarantee adverse health effects. Exposure does not automatically translate into disease, not all receipts contain bisphenols, and there is no evidence of a coordinated conspiracy to disrupt your hormones. But why willingly expose yourself to potentially harmful chemicals just to possess physical documentation that you purchased a burrito? QUESTION EVERYTHING. DECLINE THE RECEIPT. PROTECT YOUR ENDOCRINE SYSTEM. Big Paper has taken enough from us.
